A TRIBUTE TO

Srinivasa Ramanujan

An extraordinary mathematician whose work continues to influence the world of mathematics.

"An equation for me has no meaning unless it expresses a thought of God."

HIS STORY

A Life in Mathematics

Early Life

Srinivasa Ramanujan was born on 22 December 1887 in Erode, India, and grew up in Kumbakonam. From a young age, he showed exceptional mathematical ability and pursued many ideas through independent study, despite having limited formal training in advanced mathematics.

Hardy & Cambridge

In 1913, Ramanujan wrote to the British mathematician G. H. Hardy, sharing a collection of original mathematical results. Recognizing his remarkable talent, Hardy helped him travel to England in 1914, where they collaborated at the University of Cambridge.

Mathematical Work

Ramanujan made important contributions to number theory, infinite series, continued fractions, and mathematical analysis. His discoveries included remarkable identities and results that inspired further research, with some of his work receiving deeper study long after his death.

Final Years & Legacy

Ramanujan's health deteriorated during his years in England, and he returned to India in 1919. Despite his illness, he continued developing mathematical ideas. He died on 26 April 1920, aged just 32, leaving behind work that continues to influence mathematics.

MATHEMATICAL LEGACY

Ideas That Outlived Him

1729

The number 1729 is famously associated with Ramanujan and is known as the smallest number expressible as the sum of two positive cubes in two different ways.

1³ + 12³ = 9³ + 10³ = 1729

Ramanujan made significant contributions to the study of integer partitions and discovered remarkable patterns and relationships within partition numbers.
